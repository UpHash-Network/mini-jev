## Contribution supported by current evidence

Mini Jev provides a runnable local implementation with restricted probabilities, typed values, and semantic metadata, accompanied by auditable system evaluation. Its public API and browser expose typed values, distributions, concentration, and copyable request/response JSON. Candidate IDs and raw logits are available in the internal engine and research traces, not in the public response. It does not train a decision head, and it does not claim to reproduce TypeSafe's model.

The measured contribution remains the matched direct-readout/native-one-token study and presentation-sensitivity studies with explicit failure examples and call accounting. The results do not establish a latency advantage over native one-token inference, a new general finding that stability differs from correctness, or better user outcomes than another tool. The full abstract and Evidence section were preserved byte-for-byte in this revision.

The source-based platform comparison is not a head-to-head experiment. In particular, self-hosting an evaluation platform and performing local model inference are separate properties. A future comparison must hold the model, inputs, candidate information, and output semantics constant to isolate an interface effect. Comparing remote Jev predictions with local Mini Jev predictions alone would confound the interface and backend. No such human comparison has been completed.
